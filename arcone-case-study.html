The honest truth:

There was no product requirements document. No user research repository. No design system to inherit.

The client explained their vision over a single phone call, and my job was to turn that conversation into two fully realised enterprise products.

Key Design Decisions & Trade-offs

Where I held the tension — and what I chose.

Density vs. Clarity

TENSIONEnterprise users want data density. New users need breathing room.

RESOLUTIONMedium-density default with compact toggle. Power users can increase density once comfortable.

AI Visibility Spectrum

TENSIONClient wanted AI to feel central. Enterprise users distrust AI with revenue decisions.

RESOLUTIONCopilot-to-autopilot model. AI suggestions visible and explainable. Human confirmation by default.

Two Products, One Language

TENSIONDifferent users and emotional tones, but must clearly belong to the same family.

RESOLUTIONShared components & type scale. Experience360 warmer blues. Exceptions360 severity-coded colours.

The LYZA AI Assistant

TENSIONSidebar ate real estate. Chat bubble felt disconnected. Tried all three in prototypes.

RESOLUTIONHybrid: collapsible side panel + inline suggestions within workflows. Present but not pushy.

The Design Process

From Vision to Dev-Ready Screens.

Week 1–2 · Listen & Structure
Decoding the brief

Multiple client calls. Domain glossary. Structured brief. Personas & user stories.

Week 3–4 · Architecture & Flow
Mapping the product spine

IA diagrams. User flow maps. Client feedback loops. Restructured compliance priority.

Week 5–7 · Wireframes
Testing the bones

Low-fi exploration. Tested table, card, Kanban layouts. Table won for exception queue.

Week 8–14 · High-Fidelity UI
Pixel by pixel

Every screen at full fidelity. Shared component library. All states designed. Dev-ready specs.

Week 15+ · Dev Handoff
Built to ship

Figma organised page-by-page. Annotated interactions. Edge cases documented.

What I'd Do Differently

Lessons captured, written down.

01

Push Harder for User Access

Frame it as risk to the product, not a research request. Even one 30-min call would have validated personas.

02

Record Discovery Calls

Handwritten notes missed nuances. A recording would have been a better source of truth.

03

Prototype AI Earlier

Should have built rough interactive prototypes of LYZA's flows before going high-fidelity.

04

Start with the Design System

Building components as I went meant retrofitting early screens. For two products, invest week one in a shared system.

The most valuable skill in product design isn't pixel-pushing. It's turning ambiguity into structure.
